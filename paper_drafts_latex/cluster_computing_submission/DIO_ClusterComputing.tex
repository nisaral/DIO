DIO: Hybrid Cost Routing, Session Affinity, and Sound Admission Control\\
for Multi-Instance LLM Serving over Stock vLLM}

\abstract{%
Deployments often place several stock vLLM (or OpenAI-compatible) instances behind
Round-Robin or least-connections balancers. That works poorly when backends differ
in \emph{effective} service cost---queue depth, KV-cache pressure, prefix-cache
locality, or a temporarily slow peer---even when GPU SKUs match. Absolute latency
predictors learned from black-box completion telemetry are also unreliable: on dual
Tesla~T4 hardware we observe NLMS MAPE of $\sim$90--130\%, so any admission policy
that gates on predicted milliseconds is unsafe.

We present \textbf{DIO} (\texttt{dio-serve}), a non-invasive OpenAI-compatible
gateway that addresses these gaps with three complementary mechanisms.
\textbf{(A)~Hybrid cost routing} combines dual-timescale NLMS ranking (from request
end-to-end latency and token counts) with \emph{engine-exposed} Prometheus signals
scraped from each stock vLLM \texttt{/metrics} endpoint---KV-cache utilization,
waiting-queue depth, and prefix-cache hit rate---without patching engine source.
\textbf{(B)~Session/prefix affinity} elevates multi-turn and agentic locality: DIO
steers follow-up turns toward the replica already holding useful prefix state and
tracks affinity hit rate as a first-class metric.
\textbf{(C)~Sound admission under unreliable $\hat{y}$} decouples ranking from
reject/admit: \texttt{rank\_only} and \texttt{empirical} (observed-percentile)
modes avoid the thrashing of naive absolute-$\hat{y}$ gates when MAPE is high.

On dual-T4 + Qwen2.5-3B + stock vLLM ($n{=}10$ seeds), homogeneous NLMS is not
better than Round-Robin on mean p99; under controlled $\times 2$ service-time skew,
NLMS cuts p99 by $48.3\%\pm0.7\%$ vs.\ RR. Library multi-seed suites isolate hybrid
steering ($12.0\%\pm2.0\%$ p99 vs RR under engineered KV/queue pressure), affinity
stickiness ($1.00$ vs $0.60$ for RR), and admission safety (absolute rejects 100\%
of healthy traffic under poisoned $\hat{y}$; empirical/rank\_only complete all).
Open code and artifacts are released. Multi-SKU fleets are out of scope.
}

\keywords{LLM serving, multi-instance load balancing, vLLM, hybrid routing, prefix-cache affinity, admission control, NLMS, Prometheus metrics}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Motivation}
Practitioners commonly run \emph{multiple} stock vLLM~\cite{vllm} (or
SGLang/TGI~\cite{sglang,tgi,radixattention}) instances and put Nginx~\cite{nginx},
Kubernetes Services~\cite{kubernetes}, Envoy~\cite{envoy}, or Ray
Serve~\cite{rayserve} in front with Round-Robin or least-connections. Single-node
engines optimize continuous batching~\cite{orca,bullet}; they do not solve
\emph{which backend} should take the next request. Classic L7
balancers~\cite{maglev,nginx} treat backends as equal capacity units.

In production, effective cost still diverges even on identical GPU SKUs: pending
queues grow unevenly, KV-cache utilization spikes on one replica, prefix caches
favor sessions pinned to a peer, and host interference can temporarily slow one
process. The result is avoidable tail latency~\cite{dean2013tail}. Co-designed
cluster schedulers~\cite{nexussched,llumnix,loongserve} can react with deep engine
hooks or live KV migration, but many operators need a \emph{non-invasive} front
door that wraps stock OpenAI-compatible~\cite{openaiapi} engines without patches.

A second problem is quieter but equally important: online latency models learned
from black-box e2e telemetry are often accurate only as \emph{relative rankers}.
On dual Tesla~T4 we measure dual-timescale NLMS~\cite{haykin2002adaptive} MAPE of
$\sim$90--130\%. Systems that still gate admission on ``reject if $\min\hat{y}>\mathrm{SLO}$''
inherit that error and can starve healthy traffic.

\subsection{Approach}
We design \textbf{DIO} (\texttt{dio-serve}) as a thin control plane for the
multi-instance pattern (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}). The closed loop uses coarse
request telemetry (e2e latency, tokens, gateway pending) \emph{and}, when available,
stock engine metrics already exported on each vLLM Prometheus~\cite{prometheus}
endpoint---still HTTP-only, still zero source modification. Three mechanisms work
together:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
\item \textbf{Hybrid cost routing.} NLMS provides an online ranking signal from
completions; scraped KV usage, waiting requests, and prefix hit rate supply
ground-truth corrections that close part of the MAPE/telemetry gap without
sacrificing engine-agnosticism (Section~\ref{sec:hybrid}).
\item \textbf{Session/prefix affinity.} Multi-turn and agentic
workloads~\cite{autogen,langgraph} benefit when follow-ups land on the replica
holding prefix state~\cite{promptcache,hydragen,preble}. DIO makes affinity a
first-class cost term and metric, not a minor bonus (Section~\ref{sec:affinity}).
\item \textbf{Sound admission.} Ranking always uses $\arg\min S_w$; admission is a
separate policy---\texttt{rank\_only}, \texttt{empirical}, or diagnostic
\texttt{absolute}---so unreliable absolute $\hat{y}$ cannot thrash
reject/admit~\cite{clockwork,distserve} (Section~\ref{sec:admission}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Results preview}
Dual-timescale NLMS does \textbf{not} invent wins when backends are equal: on
homogeneous dual-T4, NLMS p99 is within a few percent of Round-Robin and slightly
worse (Section~\ref{sec:regimeA}). Where NLMS helps is service-time skew: under
controlled $\times 2$ e2e inflation on one real peer, NLMS reduces p99 by
$48.3\%\pm0.7\%$ vs.\ RR and beats $d{=}2$ RLS (Section~\ref{sec:regimeC}). Hybrid
metrics, affinity, and admission are evaluated as full mechanisms in
Sections~\ref{sec:eval-hybrid}--\ref{sec:eval-admission}.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Hybrid cost routing (non-invasive).} Formal joint cost that fuses
online NLMS with scraped vLLM \texttt{/metrics}; graceful degrade when scrape fails.
\item \textbf{Session/prefix affinity as multi-turn router.} Prefix-hash stickiness
plus engine prefix-hit bonus; multi-seed stickiness and latency gains vs.\ RR.
\item \textbf{Sound admission under unreliable $\hat{y}$.} Explicit rank-only /
empirical / absolute modes; quantitative disagreement under poisoned predictors.
\item \textbf{Deployable open gateway + honest multi-seed evaluation.}
Open-source \texttt{dio-serve}; dual-T4 + Qwen2.5-3B~\cite{qwen25}; no free lunch on
homogeneous peers; multi-SKU fleets out of scope.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Single-node engines and prefix/KV systems}
vLLM~\cite{vllm} popularized PagedAttention and continuous batching; alternatives
revisit memory management without paging~\cite{vattention}.
SGLang~\cite{sglang,radixattention} improves structured decoding and RadixAttention
prefix reuse. TGI~\cite{tgi} targets production APIs. Hybrid iteration policies
include Sarathi-Serve~\cite{bullet} and FastServe~\cite{fastserve}. Disaggregated
prefill/decode systems~\cite{distserve,splitwise,mooncake,memserve} optimize pipeline
stages and KV placement. Prefix/KV reuse~\cite{promptcache,hydragen,cacheblend,lmcache,preble}
raises the value of \emph{where} a request lands. These systems excel on one node or a
co-designed cluster; multi-worker routing in front of \emph{stock} engines remains
external.

\subsection{Cluster orchestrators and classical serving}
Ray Serve~\cite{rayserve} and Triton~\cite{triton} emphasize actors and model
repositories with coarse metrics. Earlier DNN serving stacks
(Clipper~\cite{clipper}, INFaaS~\cite{infaas}, Clockwork~\cite{clockwork})
established predictability and model-selection themes that LLM gateways inherit.
Orca~\cite{orca} advances iteration-level batching. AlpaServe~\cite{alpaserve} and
CoCoServe~\cite{CocoServe} focus on placement and multiplexing. M\'elange~\cite{melange}
targets heterogeneous capacity tables. NexusSched~\cite{nexussched} combines
predictive routing with engine co-design and multi-feature predictors ($O(d^2)$
updates). Llumnix~\cite{llumnix} migrates live KV state. LoongServe~\cite{loongserve}
targets long-context elastic parallelism. ServerlessLLM~\cite{serverlessllm}
optimizes cold-start loading. DIO complements these as a \emph{non-invasive
request-level front end}: dual-timescale scalar NLMS, hybrid Prometheus
scrape~\cite{prometheus}, joint cost, and stock engines only---no engine patches and
no live KV migration.

\subsection{Adaptive prediction, queues, and admission}
NLMS stability and step-size practice follow adaptive filter
theory~\cite{haykin2002adaptive,sayed2008adaptive}; recursive least squares is the
natural $O(d^2)$ comparator. Queue wait uses a batch-amortized Little-style
heuristic~\cite{littleslaw,kleinrock1975queueing}. SJF-style ranking motivates
predicted service routing~\cite{sjf}. Roofline~\cite{williams2009roofline} inspires
soft memory ceilings recast as routing penalties. Goodput- and SLO-aware LLM
serving~\cite{distserve,sloawarellm,sloaware2026} motivates treating admission as a
first-class policy. Simulation frameworks such as Vidur~\cite{vidur} characterize
offline capacity; DIO targets online multi-instance ranking under weak absolute
models.

\section{System design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}
DIO separates a \textbf{control plane} from a \textbf{data plane} of unmodified
engines (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}). Clients send OpenAI-compatible
\texttt{/v1/chat/completions} (or \texttt{/v1/completions}) to DIO. The scheduler
selects a backend, proxies the request, and updates per-worker models from measured
end-to-end latency and token counts. Product path: \texttt{dio-serve} (FastAPI +
Python scheduler). Optional research path: Go manager with gRPC workers.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\columnwidth]{system_Design.png}
\caption{DIO multi-instance gateway. Clients talk OpenAI HTTP to DIO. The control
plane ranks backends with dual-timescale NLMS, hybrid Prometheus scrape from each
stock vLLM, session affinity, and decoupled admission. Data-plane engines are
unmodified.}
\label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Telemetry contract.}
The closed loop uses four channels: (i)~wall-clock e2e latency through the gateway;
(ii)~token counts from response \texttt{usage} or a tokenizer estimate; (iii)~per-backend
\texttt{pending} depth inside DIO; (iv)~optional hybrid scrape of each backend's
\texttt{/metrics}. Soft/hard VRAM terms can be config-driven or inferred from KV usage.
We do \textbf{not} claim continuous NVML SM/power/temperature telemetry, and we do not
patch engine source---in contrast to co-designed control planes that require deep
hooks~\cite{nexussched,llumnix}.

\subsection{Dual-timescale NLMS latency model}
\label{sec:nlms}
For worker $w$ and approximate token count $N$, we model
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_w = s_w N + b_w,
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where $s_w$ is ms/token and $b_w$ is fixed overhead. On completion with residual
$e = y - \hat{y}$, NLMS updates
\begin{equation}
s \leftarrow s + \mu \frac{e}{N}, \qquad
b \leftarrow b + \mu_b e,
\end{equation}
with dual rates $\mu_{\mathrm{fast}}{=}0.1$, $\mu_{\mathrm{slow}}{=}0.01$,
$\mu_b{=}0.005$, and
\begin{equation}
s^{\mathrm{eff}} = \alpha s^{\mathrm{fast}} + (1-\alpha) s^{\mathrm{slow}}, \quad \alpha{=}0.8.
\label{eq:dual}
\end{equation}
Cold-start priors are $s_0{=}2.0$\,ms/token, $b_0{=}150$\,ms. We distinguish
(i)~$O(1)$ arithmetic per update from (ii)~time-to-usable ranking: typically
\textbf{15--20 completions per worker} before slopes leave priors.
Figure~\ref{fig:nlms_conv} illustrates dual-timescale response to an injected slope
jump in simulation. Token feature $N$ prefers Hugging Face tokenizer counts plus
planned \texttt{max\_tokens}; fallback is $\lfloor|\mathrm{prompt}|/4\rfloor+\texttt{max\_tokens}$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\columnwidth]{fig_nlms_convergence_throttle.png}
\caption{NLMS dual-timescale adaptation under an injected true-slope jump at request
80 (simulation). $s^{\mathrm{fast}}$ reacts first; $s^{\mathrm{slow}}$ lags. Synthetic
illustration, not dual-T4 wall-clock traces.}
\label{fig:nlms_conv}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Baseline joint cost}
\label{sec:cost-baseline}
Absent hybrid metrics, each healthy worker scores
\begin{equation}
S_w^{\mathrm{base}} =
\mathrm{wait}_w + \hat{t}_w + \mathrm{tierCost}_{r,w} + \mathrm{vramCost}_w
- \mathrm{cacheBonus}_w,
\label{eq:base}
\end{equation}
with $\hat{t}_w = s^{\mathrm{eff}}_w N + b_w$,
$\mathrm{wait}_w = (Q_w/B)\bar{t}_w$ ($B{=}8$), soft VRAM penalty when free VRAM
${<}4$\,GB, hard exclusion when free VRAM ${<}2.4$\,GB and $N{>}1000$, and tier
mismatch soft cost $500$\,ms (hard block for large-on-small). Route to
$\arg\min_w S_w$ in $O(|W|)$ time. Strategies also include Round-Robin,
Least-Loaded, static slopes, and $d{=}2$ RLS as head-to-head baselines under the
\emph{same} cost shell when applicable.

\subsection{Implementation notes}
\label{sec:impl}
\texttt{dio serve -b e0=URL0 -b e1=URL1} fronts stock engines. Background asyncio
tasks scrape \texttt{/metrics} (default interval $1$\,s) and probe health. Debug
endpoints expose slopes, MAPE, routes, engine snapshots, affinity counters, and
admission diagnostics: \texttt{/debug/metrics}, \texttt{/debug/engine},
\texttt{/debug/affinity}, \texttt{/debug/admission}. Control-plane pick overhead is
$\sim$10--15\,$\mu$s (Section~\ref{sec:overhead}).

\section{Hybrid cost routing with engine metrics}
\label{sec:hybrid}

\subsection{Problem: black-box ranking misses engine ground truth}
NLMS observes only gateway e2e latency and tokens. That signal is delayed, noisy,
and confounded by batching. Meanwhile stock vLLM already exports internal state
via Prometheus~\cite{prometheus}: GPU KV-cache utilization, number of waiting and
running requests, and prefix-cache hit counters. Prior multi-instance balancers
either ignore these signals or require invasive co-design~\cite{nexussched,llumnix}.
DIO's hybrid design is deliberately coarse: \textbf{scrape what the engine already
publishes}, fold it into the same millisecond-scale cost used for ranking, and
keep zero source modification.

\subsection{Metric scrape and snapshot}
For each backend $i$ with base URL $U_i$ and metrics path (default
\texttt{/metrics}), a background task issues \texttt{GET $U_i$/metrics} and parses
Prometheus exposition text. Metric name variants across vLLM versions are handled
(e.g.\ \texttt{vllm:gpu\_cache\_usage\_perc} vs.\ \texttt{vllm:kv\_cache\_usage\_perc};
prefix hit rate from a direct gauge or from hits/queries counters). Each scrape
yields a snapshot
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}_w = (\mathrm{KV}_w,\, W_w,\, R_w,\, \mathrm{Hit}_w,\, \mathrm{ok}_w),
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{KV}_w\in[0,1]$, $W_w$ and $R_w$ are waiting/running request counts,
$\mathrm{Hit}_w\in[0,1]$ is prefix-cache hit rate, and $\mathrm{ok}_w$ is false on
HTTP/parse failure. On failure, hybrid terms contribute zero (graceful degrade to
NLMS-only ranking). Scrape interval defaults to $1$\,s---fast enough to track KV
pressure, slow enough not to contend with decode.

\subsection{Hybrid joint cost}
When $\mathrm{ok}_w$ is true, the full score is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S_w ={}& \mathrm{wait}_w + \hat{t}_w + \mathrm{tierCost}_{r,w} + \mathrm{vramCost}_w \\
&+ c_{\mathrm{kv}}\,\mathrm{KV}_w + c_{q}\,W_w
- \mathrm{cacheBonus}_w - c_{p}\,\mathrm{Hit}_w.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:hybrid}
\end{equation}
Default coefficients (same ms units as other terms): $c_{\mathrm{kv}}{=}800$,
$c_{q}{=}50$ per waiting request, $c_{p}{=}150$. Additionally, if
$\mathrm{KV}_w{>}0.95$ and $N{>}500$, worker $w$ is hard-blocked for that request
(engine-reported near-full KV). Free-VRAM estimates may be updated as
$\mathrm{free}_w \approx \mathrm{total}_w(1-\mathrm{KV}_w)$ when totals are known.

\paragraph{What hybrid is not.}
Hybrid cost is \emph{not} a claim of SM utilization, power, or temperature
awareness. It is also not a substitute for NLMS: when backends differ only in
learned service slope and metrics look similar, NLMS ranking still dominates. The
novelty is the combination---lightweight online ranker + non-invasive engine
ground truth---still deployable as a stock gateway.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Hybrid engine scrape (background)}
\label{alg:scrape}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require backends $B$, interval $\Delta$
\Loop
  \ForAll{backend $b\in B$}
    \State $T \leftarrow$ HTTP GET $b.\mathrm{metrics\_url}$ with timeout
    \If{$T$ ok}
      \State $\mathcal{E}_b \leftarrow$ ParsePrometheus$(T)$
      \State Scheduler.SetEngineMetrics$(b,\mathcal{E}_b)$
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \State sleep $\Delta$
\EndLoop
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{DIO worker selection (hybrid + affinity + admission)}
\label{alg:dio}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require request $r$, workers $W$, admission mode $m$, snapshots $\{\mathcal{E}_w\}$
\State $N \leftarrow$ TokenFeature$(r)$; $w^\star\leftarrow\bot$; $S_{\min}\leftarrow\infty$
\ForAll{$w\in W$ healthy and tier-feasible}
  \State $\hat{t}_w \leftarrow$ Predict$(w,N)$
  \If{VRAM/KV hard-blocked$(w,N,\mathcal{E}_w)$} \textbf{continue}
  \EndIf
  \State $S_w \leftarrow$ wait$+\hat{t}_w+$tier$+$vram$+c_{\mathrm{kv}}\mathrm{KV}_w+c_q W_w$
  \Statex \hspace{\algorithmicindent}$-$cacheBonus$(r,w)-c_p\mathrm{Hit}_w$
  \If{$S_w < S_{\min}$} $S_{\min}\leftarrow S_w$; $w^\star\leftarrow w$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\If{$w^\star=\bot$ \textbf{or} AdmissionReject$(S_{\min},m)$}
  \State \Return HTTP 503 with Retry-After
\EndIf
\State RecordPrefixAffinity$(r,w^\star)$; \Return $w^\star$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Session and prefix-cache affinity}
\label{sec:affinity}

\subsection{Problem: multi-turn locality is underserved by RR}
Round-Robin and least-connections scatter multi-turn sessions across replicas.
Each turn that lands on a cold replica re-prefills shared system prompts and agent
context, missing in-engine prefix caches~\cite{radixattention,promptcache,hydragen}.
Cache-aware and prompt-scheduling systems~\cite{preble,cacheblend} show large gains
when locality is preserved, but they often assume co-designed clusters. DIO targets
the common case: several stock replicas behind one OpenAI gateway, multi-turn or
agentic clients~\cite{autogen,langgraph}, no engine patches.

\subsection{Affinity mechanism}
On each admitted request with prompt text $p$, DIO computes a stable prefix hash
over the first $256$ characters:
\begin{equation}
h(p) = \mathrm{hash}(p[0{:}256]) \bmod 2^{32}.
\end{equation}
A map $M: h\mapsto w$ records the last worker that successfully served that prefix.
When scoring worker $w$, if $M[h(p)]=w$, a cache affinity bonus
$\mathrm{cacheBonus}_w = C$ (default $C{=}200$\,ms; raised in multi-turn experiments)
is subtracted from $S_w$. Independently, the hybrid term $c_p\mathrm{Hit}_w$ prefers
workers the \emph{engine} reports as currently caching well. After admission, DIO
sets $M[h(p)]\leftarrow w^\star$.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
DIO maintains $\mathrm{affinity\_decisions}$ and $\mathrm{affinity\_hits}$ (true when
the chosen worker already owned the prefix). Exposed hit rate
$\mathrm{affinity\_hits}/\mathrm{affinity\_decisions}$ is the primary stickiness
metric for multi-turn evaluation. When live vLLM metrics are available, operators
can also compare engine-reported prefix hit rates under DIO vs.\ RR.

\paragraph{Relationship to in-engine caching.}
Affinity is request-level stickiness complementary to RadixAttention~\cite{radixattention}:
DIO increases the chance that the engine's own cache is warm; it does not implement
a second cache. Ablating \texttt{no\_cache} disables the bonus for sensitivity
studies.

\section{Sound admission under unreliable latency prediction}
\label{sec:admission}

\subsection{Problem: absolute gates trust a weak model}
Many predictive schedulers implicitly assume $\hat{y}$ is calibrated enough to
compare against an SLO in absolute milliseconds. Section~\ref{sec:mape} shows that
assumption fails for NLMS on dual-T4 (MAPE $\sim$90--130\%). Cold-start priors and
batching can make $S_w$ larger than any reasonable SLO even when true latency is
fine. Gating on $\min_w S_w > \mathrm{SLO}$ then rejects healthy traffic---or, if
priors are optimistic, admits work that will miss the SLO. Predictability-first
serving~\cite{clockwork} and goodput-oriented LLM systems~\cite{distserve,sloawarellm}
motivate treating admission as a policy that must not inherit model bias.

\subsection{Three admission modes}
Ranking is always $\arg\min_w S_w$ among feasible workers. Admission is separate:

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.2em]
\item[\texttt{rank\_only}]
Never reject for SLO based on $S_w$. Only hard VRAM/tier/KV blocks apply. NLMS is
used purely for ranking. Preferred for routing A/B tests and when the operator
handles overload externally.
\item[\texttt{empirical} (default)]
Maintain a rolling window of observed e2e latencies (default length $64$). After
warm-up ($\ge 8$ samples), compute the $p$-th percentile (default $p{=}95$). Reject
only if that empirical tail exceeds the SLO \emph{and} the best current score lies
in the worst quartile of available worker scores. Cold-start never rejects on
$\hat{y}$ alone.
\item[\texttt{absolute} (legacy / diagnostic)]
Reject if $\min_w S_w > \mathrm{SLO}$. Sensitive to MAPE and cold-start scale.
Retained so operators can quantify how often absolute would disagree with the
active mode.
\end{description}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{AdmissionReject$(S_{\min}, m)$}
\label{alg:admission}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require best score $S_{\min}$, mode $m$, SLO $L$, recent e2e window $\mathcal{Y}$
\If{$m=\texttt{rank\_only}$} \Return false
\EndIf
\If{$m=\texttt{empirical}$}
  \If{$|\mathcal{Y}|<8$} \Return false \Comment{warm-up: do not trust $\hat{y}$}
  \EndIf
  \State $q \leftarrow$ Percentile$(\mathcal{Y},p)$
  \State $Q_{75} \leftarrow$ Percentile of current feasible scores at 75\%
  \State \Return $(q > L) \land (S_{\min} \ge Q_{75})$
\EndIf
\State \Return $(S_{\min} > L)$ \Comment{\texttt{absolute}}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Diagnostics.}
Counters \texttt{would\_reject\_absolute}, \texttt{would\_admit\_absolute}, and
\texttt{absolute\_vs\_active\_disagree} record, on every pick, whether absolute mode
would have made a different decision. These are the primary paper metrics for
admission safety (Section~\ref{sec:eval-admission}). Rejected requests return HTTP
503 with \texttt{Retry-After}.

\section{Experimental evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Hardware and software (real GPU).}
Dual NVIDIA Tesla T4, stock vLLM~\cite{vllm}, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct~\cite{qwen25},
Hugging Face tokenizer, \texttt{dio-serve} gateway. Workshop suite:
\texttt{run\_workshop\_final\_suite.py}.

\paragraph{Regimes (non-interchangeable).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Regime A --- homogeneous dual-T4 (real):}
  identical peers, $\texttt{max\_tokens}{=}32$, $n{=}10$ seeds $\times$ 30 requests.
\item \textbf{Regime B --- legacy Locust pilot (secondary):}
  longer-decode ShareGPT~\cite{sharegpt}/arXiv/Azure-style traces, single seed;
  absolute seconds not mixed with Regime A ms.
\item \textbf{Regime C --- service-time skew:}
  (i) CPU multi-seed simulation $n{=}10$; (ii) real dual-T4 with e1 behind
  delay-proxy $\times 2$, $n{=}10$.
\item \textbf{A/B/C mechanism suites (library multi-seed):}
  hybrid pressure, multi-turn affinity, admission under poisoned $\hat{y}$;
  $n{=}10$ unless noted (\texttt{run\_abc\_experiments.py}). Runnable without GPUs;
  isolate mechanism correctness complementary to dual-T4 cells.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Baselines and metrics.}
Round-Robin (RR), Least-Loaded (LL), RLS~\cite{haykin2002adaptive} ($d{=}2$,
$\lambda{=}0.99$) under the same engines where applicable. Primary metrics:
p50/p99 e2e mean$\pm$std; MAPE; fraction to backend e0; affinity hit rate /
stickiness; admission reject rate, completions, absolute-vs-active disagreement.
We do not claim a matched multi-node Orca~\cite{orca} or vLLM-distributed bake-off.

\subsection{Evaluation of hybrid cost routing}
\label{sec:eval-hybrid}

\paragraph{Setup.}
To isolate contribution A without multi-SKU hardware, we run a library multi-seed
suite ($n{=}10$ seeds $\times$ 120 requests). Two workers share the same true service
law $y=2N+100+\epsilon$. One worker is labeled \emph{hot} via injected engine
snapshots (high $\mathrm{KV}$, high $W$) and accrues extra queue delay when pending
builds; the other is \emph{cool}. We compare Hybrid (NLMS + hybrid terms), NLMS-only
(no engine metrics), and Round-Robin.

\paragraph{Results.}
Figure~\ref{fig:hybridA} and Table~\ref{tab:hybrid} show that hybrid and NLMS-only
both learn to avoid the hot worker once queue delay appears in e2e feedback (frac
cool $1.00$), while RR remains balanced ($0.50$). Hybrid achieves p99
$257\pm3$\,ms vs.\ RR $292\pm6$\,ms: \textbf{$12.0\%\pm2.0\%$ p99 reduction vs.\ RR}.
NLMS-only reaches similar p99 once feedback reveals the hot queue; hybrid's value is
\emph{earlier and more direct} use of engine ground truth---KV/queue terms that do
not wait for e2e residuals---and hard KV blocks under near-full cache. On live
clusters, scrape latency ($\sim$1\,s) is far below decode timescales, so hybrid terms
act as a continuous correction while NLMS adapts slopes.

\subsection{Evaluation of session/prefix affinity}
\label{sec:eval-affinity}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Library multi-seed multi-turn workload: $n{=}10$ seeds, $20$ sessions $\times$ $5$
turns, long shared system prefix per session (agent-style context). Workers are
near-homogeneous; NLMS+affinity uses cache bonus $C{=}400$\,ms and engine prefix-hit
bonus; RR uses neither. Stickiness is the fraction of turns remaining on the
session's home worker. Affinity hit rate is DIO's internal counter (first turn of a
session cannot be a hit). Synthetic engine hit rates favor the last worker of the
session to mimic warm prefix caches.

\paragraph{Results.}
Figure~\ref{fig:affinityB} and Table~\ref{tab:affinity}: NLMS+affinity achieves
affinity hit rate $0.80\pm0.00$ and session stickiness $1.00$ vs.\ RR stickiness

$190\pm4$\,ms (affinity) under a cache-hit latency model (warm turns $0.7\times$ cold).

\subsection{Evaluation of sound admission}
\label{sec:eval-admission}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Library multi-seed suite ($n{=}10\times 120$) with a single worker. True latency is
mild: $y=120+1.5N+|\epsilon|$ and always under SLO $L{=}500$\,ms. NLMS slopes are
\emph{poisoned} ($s{=}40$, $b{=}800$) so absolute $S_w\gg L$ despite healthy true
service---the high-MAPE regime of Section~\ref{sec:mape}. We compare
\texttt{absolute}, \texttt{empirical}, and \texttt{rank\_only}.

\paragraph{Results.}
Figure~\ref{fig:admissionC} and Table~\ref{tab:admission}: \texttt{absolute} rejects
\textbf{100\%} of requests (0 completions)---healthy traffic is starved because the
gate trusts broken $\hat{y}$. \texttt{empirical} and \texttt{rank\_only} reject
\textbf{0\%}, complete all 120 requests, and keep goodput $1.0$. They also record
$120$ absolute-vs-active disagreements per seed: every pick is one where absolute
would have erred. Under genuine overload (empirical tail hot \emph{and} scores in the
worst quartile), empirical still rejects; the point is not ``never shed load'' but
``do not shed load because the model is mis-scaled.''

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{When does each mechanism matter?}
NLMS ranking alone helps under \emph{observed service skew}
(Section~\ref{sec:regimeC}) and not under matched peers
(Section~\ref{sec:regimeA}). Hybrid metrics matter when engine pressure (KV, waiting
queue) is visible before e2e residuals fully reflect it
(Section~\ref{sec:eval-hybrid}). Affinity matters for multi-turn locality without
requiring hetero hardware (Section~\ref{sec:eval-affinity}). Admission mode matters
whenever MAPE is high enough that absolute $S_w$ is not a safe SLO proxy
(Section~\ref{sec:eval-admission}). Operators should enable hybrid scrape whenever
\texttt{/metrics} is available, keep affinity on for chat/agent workloads, and
prefer \texttt{empirical} or \texttt{rank\_only} over \texttt{absolute}.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Telemetry depth.} Hybrid scrape uses stock vLLM exports, not continuous
NVML SM/power/temperature.
\item \textbf{Skew model.} Real GPU skew uses delay-proxy on identical T4s, not
physical multi-SKU fleets.
\item \textbf{Live dual-T4 A/B/C cells.} Affinity and hybrid p99 under concurrent
multi-turn load on dual-T4 remain an extension; library suites establish mechanism
correctness and multi-seed statistics.
\item \textbf{Baselines.} RR/LL/RLS on the same vLLM pair; not multi-node Orca or
vLLM-distributed.
\item \textbf{Workload.} Short sequential chat probes dominate dual-T4 multi-seed
cells; concurrent ShareGPT multi-seed is incomplete.
\item \textbf{Out of scope.} Prefill/decode disaggregation co-design, multimodal
split inference, live KV migration.
\end{enumerate}

%%=============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

DIO (\texttt{dio-serve}) is a non-invasive multi-instance gateway for stock
vLLM-class engines. We contribute three full mechanisms: (A)~hybrid cost routing that
fuses dual-timescale NLMS ranking with scraped engine KV/queue/prefix metrics;
(B)~session/prefix affinity for multi-turn and agentic workloads; and (C)~admission
control that does not trust absolute $\hat{y}$ when MAPE is high. Multi-seed dual-T4
results show no free lunch when backends match and clear p99 gains when one peer is
effectively slower. Mechanism suites show hybrid steering, affinity stickiness, and
absolute-vs-empirical admission disagreement. Together these form a deployable
systems stack for common multi-vLLM deployments---without engine patches, multi-SKU
hardware, or full GPU telemetry. Open code and artifacts accompany the paper.
