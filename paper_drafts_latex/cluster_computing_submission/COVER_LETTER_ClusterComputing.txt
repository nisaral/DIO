Motivation and fit
------------------
Multi-instance vLLM (or SGLang/TGI) deployments commonly place Nginx, Envoy,
Kubernetes Services, or Ray Serve in front of independent replicas with
Round-Robin. Those balancers treat peers as interchangeable, yet queue depth,
KV-cache pressure, and transient per-replica slowdowns diverge in practice.
Co-designed cluster systems address this with deep engine hooks, disaggregated
prefill/decode pools, or live KV migration. Many deployments need a non-invasive
OpenAI-compatible front door instead. That setting sits in the journal's scope on
distributed systems, scheduling, and high-performance application stacks.

DIO (dio-serve) is a thin gateway requiring no engine patches. It ranks backends
with dual-timescale NLMS learned from black-box end-to-end latency and token
counts, optionally folds in each engine's existing Prometheus /metrics, and
exposes optional session affinity and admission modes. The paper's focus is online
relative service-rate learning for heterogeneous unmodified vLLM replicas --- not
a claim to replace Kubernetes-native or co-designed serving stacks.


Principal results
-----------------
  - Dual Tesla T4 (n=10 seeds): near Round-Robin parity on matched peers;
    controlled 2x end-to-end latency inflation: mean p99 improves by 48.3% (SD 0.7%) vs RR.
  - Physical heterogeneity --- Tesla T4 + NVIDIA A30 (primary n=5 seeds, sequential
    mixed max_tokens, no injected delay): NLMS and d=2 RLS both improve mean p99 vs RR
    by ~33% (5/5 wins each; Wilcoxon NLMS vs RLS p=1.00). We do not claim NLMS beats
    RLS on accuracy; they have similar mean p99, with NLMS showing lower routing-fraction
    variability (NLMS 89.5% +/- 2.1% to A30; RLS 79.5% +/- 37.5% with one seed
    flipping to the T4). Offline OLS is a post-hoc check (~2.82x slope ratio vs 2.68x
    fixed-length end-to-end probe), not a pre-deployment profile. Offset five-seed cell reproduces A30-directed routing.
  - Admission boundary: empirical and rank-only complete all requests under a tight
    SLO; absolute-prediction gating rejects ~40% (reject rate 0.40 +/- 0.21).


On interpretation
-----------------
We are direct about scope. Regime D is sequential single-in-flight load on one
model and one GPU pair; concurrent multi-in-flight heterogeneous ranking and a
Production Stack GPU bake-off remain future work. NLMS and RLS are competitive on
central p99 at n=5; NLMS shows lower seed-to-seed dispersion and cleaner routing
in this cell. Optional affinity and metric scrape are operational extensions, not
independent performance claims for the primary contribution.
