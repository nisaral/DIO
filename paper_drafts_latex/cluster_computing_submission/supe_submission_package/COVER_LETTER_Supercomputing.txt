Motivation and fit
------------------
Operators commonly place several stock vLLM (or similar) engines behind a simple
Round-Robin or least-connections balancer. Peers are not equal in practice:
queues, KV-cache pressure, and temporary slowdowns create avoidable tail latency.
Co-designed cluster systems can fix this with deep engine hooks or live KV
migration, but many deployments need a non-invasive OpenAI-compatible front door.

DIO (dio-serve) is a thin multi-instance gateway that combines:
  (A) hybrid cost routing — dual-timescale NLMS ranking plus non-invasive scrape
      of each engine’s Prometheus /metrics (KV usage, waiting requests, prefix hits);
  (B) session/prefix affinity for multi-turn and agent traffic, with reported
      affinity hit rate and stickiness;
  (C) sound admission that does not trust absolute latency predictions when MAPE
      is high (rank-only / empirical defaults; absolute mode shown as a diagnostic).

These themes match The Journal of Supercomputing’s scope in parallel and
distributed systems, scheduling, and high-performance application stacks.

Main results (dual Tesla T4, Qwen2.5-3B, stock vLLM; multi-seed n=10 where noted)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  • Homogeneous short probes: near Round-Robin parity (stable, no spurious wins).
  • Controlled ×2 service-time asymmetry on real dual-T4: NLMS cuts p99 by about
    half vs Round-Robin (48.3% ± 0.7%) and beats RLS.
  • Real multi-turn hybrid metrics: strong tail gains on most seeds (8/10; median
    ~52% better p99 vs RR; Wilcoxon p≈0.01), clearly above hybrid-off.
  • Affinity: essentially perfect stickiness vs RR under load; hit rate ~0.91–0.93.
  • Admission: empirical/rank_only keep all traffic under a tight SLO; absolute
    predicted-latency gating over-rejects (~40%).

The paper targets the common stock multi-instance pattern (no engine patches) with
multi-seed dual-T4 evidence and open-source artifacts (https://github.com/nisaral/DIO).
