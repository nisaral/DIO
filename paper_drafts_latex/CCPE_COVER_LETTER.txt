Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, “DIO: Hybrid Cost Routing, Session Affinity, and
Calibration-Robust Admission for Multi-Instance LLM Serving over Stock vLLM,” for
publication in Concurrency and Computation: Practice and Experience.

The manuscript presents an engine-agnostic control-plane gateway for deployments
that already run multiple OpenAI-compatible LLM servers. DIO learns relative
backend service capacity from black-box request completions, optionally fuses
read-only Prometheus telemetry, preserves multi-turn prefix locality, and separates
latency ranking from admission decisions when absolute prediction is poorly
calibrated. The emphasis is on practice and experience: no engine fork or custom
kernel is required, failure and restart behavior are documented, and the complete
implementation and committed result summaries are public.

The revised evaluation includes a real five-seed Tesla T4/Tesla A30 deployment,
where NLMS reduces p99 latency by 33.3% versus Round-Robin and wins all five paired
seeds. It also reports a negative operational result: Least-Loaded routes all
traffic to the T4 in this heterogeneous setting and is 22.7% worse than
Round-Robin. A paired-bootstrap reanalysis, artifact map, and explicit limitations
are included. A separate Production Stack smoke test is presented only as a
compatibility case study, not as an unsupported performance comparison.

The authors declare no external funding and no competing interests. The manuscript
is not under consideration elsewhere. Source code and experimental artifacts are
available at https://github.com/nisaral/DIO; a versioned archival release will be
deposited in Zenodo and cited by DOI before publication.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,
